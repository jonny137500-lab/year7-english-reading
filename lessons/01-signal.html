YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

The Signal on the Headland

Inference, atmosphere and symbolism

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Central reading idea

A signal on a headland is more than an object or action: its meaning depends on who sends it, who sees it, and what is at risk. Read the surrounding details before deciding what the signal represents.

How to infer

Inference combines a textual clue with a reasonable conclusion. For example, a character repeatedly checking the horizon may suggest anxiety or expectation. The text may never state the feeling directly; the repeated behaviour is the evidence.

Notice the atmosphere

Writers create atmosphere through setting, weather, sound, light and sentence rhythm. Short sentences can quicken pace. Harsh sounds and threatening weather may create tension, while stillness can create suspense.

Think about symbolism

A light or signal can suggest hope, warning, rescue, isolation or communication. Do not choose a symbolic meaning simply because it sounds clever. Ask which interpretation best fits the events and the character's response.

A strong answer

The repeated description of the light as ___ suggests ___. This creates a feeling of ___ because ___. The detail also hints that ___.

Common mistake

Do not write only 'the setting is scary'. Name the exact word or detail, identify its effect, and explain why it suits this moment.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
