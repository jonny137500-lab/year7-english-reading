The Map in the Attic

Mystery, foreshadowing and character

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Follow the discovery

In a mystery, the order in which information is revealed matters. Record what the character knows at the beginning, what the map changes, and which questions remain unanswered.

Foreshadowing

A small earlier detail may prepare the reader for a later event. An unusual mark, a locked drawer or a character avoiding a question can become significant. A clue is not proof until later information confirms its role.

Character through action

A character's choices reveal more than labels such as 'brave' or 'curious'. Notice hesitation, secrecy, risk-taking and changes in behaviour. Consider why the character acts this way.

Setting as a clue

An attic can suggest forgotten history, family secrets or neglect, but these are possibilities rather than automatic meanings. Connect the setting to concrete objects and events in the passage.

A strong answer

The map changes ___ because ___. The earlier detail about ___ foreshadows ___, leading the reader to expect ___.

Common mistake

Do not confuse suspense with surprise. Suspense makes the reader anticipate an outcome; surprise overturns an expectation.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
