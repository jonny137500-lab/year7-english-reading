When the Data Disagreed

Non-fiction reasoning and data limitations

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Check the question being measured

Two studies may disagree because they define a term differently, use different groups or collect information at different times. Before deciding which is wrong, compare their methods.

Sample and bias

Ask how participants were selected, how many were included and who was missing. A small or unrepresentative sample may not support a broad conclusion.

Mean, median and outliers

An average can hide variation. A few unusually high or low values may shift the mean, while the median can better represent a skewed set of results. Look at the spread as well as the centre.

Uncertainty

Measurements have limits. A difference may be too small to be meaningful, or results may vary across groups. Responsible writers state limitations instead of pretending data is perfect.

A strong answer

The results differ because the studies use ___ and ___. This means the evidence supports ___ more strongly than ___, but further data would be needed to establish ___.

Common mistake

Do not select whichever number supports your preferred opinion. Evaluate how each result was produced and what conclusion it can legitimately support.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
