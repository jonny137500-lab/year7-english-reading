YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

The Last Patch of Grass

Setting, imagery and competing needs

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Setting creates meaning

A small green space can be important because of what happens there: play, shade, habitat, rest or community contact. Explain its role in the story rather than merely describing its appearance.

Imagery and word choice

Sensory details help readers imagine a place. Notice contrasts such as concrete against grass, noise against quiet, or heat against shade. Contrast can reveal conflict and make a change more noticeable.

Competing needs

Characters may value the same place for different reasons. One may prioritise housing or development; another may prioritise recreation or wildlife. Explain each viewpoint fairly before evaluating the conflict.

Tone

Tone is the writer's attitude, conveyed through diction and detail. A reflective tone may invite readers to consider loss; an urgent tone may encourage action. Support tone labels with words from the text.

A strong answer

The contrast between ___ and ___ emphasises ___. This helps the reader understand why ___ matters to ___.

Common mistake

Avoid assuming that the writer rejects all development or supports all preservation. Look at the actual position and qualifications in the text.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
