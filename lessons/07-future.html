YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

A Message from the Future

Speculation, plausibility and tone

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Separate what is known

Identify what the narrator knows, what they believe and what they predict. A prediction is not a fact, even when it is expressed confidently.

Test plausibility

Consider whether the message's details fit the world established by the passage. Look for contradictions, unexplained technology, impossible knowledge or suspiciously convenient timing.

Narrative voice

A message may sound urgent, calm, frightened or persuasive. Word choice, sentence length and direct address shape the reader's response and the narrator's credibility.

Ambiguity

Writers sometimes leave more than one explanation possible. A strong interpretation acknowledges uncertainty and uses clues to explain why one reading is more likely.

A strong answer

The message appears ___ because ___. The detail ___ makes the claim more/less plausible, while ___ leaves the reader uncertain about ___.

Common mistake

Do not present a theory as the only possible answer when the text deliberately leaves questions open.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
