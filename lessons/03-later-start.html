YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

A Case for the Later Start

Persuasion, claims and balanced evaluation

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Identify the contention

The contention is the writer's main position. State it in one sentence. Then identify the reasons offered to support it; reasons are not the same as examples or evidence.

Track persuasive choices

Look for emotive language, rhetorical questions, inclusive language, repetition, expert opinion, comparisons and statistics. Explain how each choice is intended to influence a particular audience.

Check the evidence

A statistic sounds precise, but ask what was measured, how many people were included and whether the data represents the group being discussed. A personal anecdote can illustrate an issue but cannot prove it is universal.

Consider the counterargument

A balanced evaluation recognises a reasonable opposing concern, such as transport, family schedules or after-school activities. Then judge whether the writer responds effectively.

A strong answer

The writer's contention is ___. The use of ___ is intended to persuade ___ by ___. However, the argument is less convincing when ___.

Common mistake

Do not say a technique is persuasive simply because it exists. Explain the audience, intended effect and whether the evidence makes the argument credible.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
