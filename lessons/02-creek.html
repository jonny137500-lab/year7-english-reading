YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

The Creek That Disappeared

Cause and effect, evidence and viewpoint

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Central reading idea

A disappearing creek invites the reader to separate observation from explanation. The fact that water is missing is an observation; why it happened is a claim that needs evidence.

Build a cause-and-effect chain

Look for events in order: a change in rainfall, a new drain, a change in land use, a blocked channel, then an effect on plants or animals. Do not assume that two events occurring together proves one caused the other.

Evaluate evidence

Specific measurements, dates, repeated observations and expert explanations are usually stronger than vague comments. Ask who collected the evidence, when it was collected and whether another explanation is possible.

Viewpoint

A farmer, resident, scientist and local council may focus on different consequences. Viewpoint does not automatically make a speaker unreliable; it helps explain what they notice and value.

A strong answer

The passage links ___ with ___ by showing ___. This supports the idea that ___, although the evidence does not prove ___.

Common mistake

Avoid confusing a possible cause with a proven cause. Use cautious language such as 'suggests', 'may have contributed' or 'is consistent with' when certainty is limited.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
