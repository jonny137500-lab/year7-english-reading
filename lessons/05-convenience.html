YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

The Cost of Convenience

Argument, statistics and bias

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Look beyond the title

A text about convenience may examine hidden costs: packaging, energy, labour, waste, time or environmental effects. Identify which costs are measured and which are simply mentioned.

Read numbers carefully

Check units, time periods, sample sizes and comparisons. 'A 20% increase' is incomplete without knowing the starting value. A large number may be less meaningful than a fair comparison.

Spot loaded language

Words such as 'wasteful', 'essential' or 'careless' can guide the reader's judgement. Ask whether the writer supports these words with evidence or uses them to frame the issue emotionally.

Separate evidence from opinion

A measured result is evidence; a judgement about what society should do is an opinion or recommendation. Strong arguments connect the two transparently.

A strong answer

The figure ___ shows ___. It supports the writer's claim that ___, but it does not by itself establish ___.

Common mistake

Do not treat correlation as causation, or assume that one example represents every situation.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
