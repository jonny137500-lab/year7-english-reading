YEAR 7 ENGLISH · READING GUIDE

The Photograph That Wasn't There

Visual literacy, reliability and ambiguity

How to use this guide

Return to the passage and find the detail that supports each idea. The examples below are frameworks for thinking, not pre-written answers to copy.

Read an image systematically

Start with literal observations: people, objects, setting, framing, light, focus and what is outside the frame. Then infer meaning. Do not jump directly from an image to a dramatic explanation.

Framing and omission

A photograph records a selected view, not the whole event. Cropping, angle and timing can shape what viewers notice. Ask what might exist beyond the frame.

Reliability

A photograph may be genuine yet incomplete or misleading. Consider its source, date, context, editing and caption. A caption is a claim about the image, not automatic proof.

Ambiguity and absence

If an expected person or object is missing, explore possible explanations and rank them using clues. Absence can create mystery, but it does not prove what happened.

A strong answer

The photograph draws attention to ___ through ___. The omission of ___ may suggest ___, although another explanation is ___.

Common mistake

Do not claim that a photograph proves intention or motive unless the surrounding evidence supports that conclusion.

Before you submit an answer

  • Have I answered the exact question?
  • Have I selected a relevant detail from the passage?
  • Have I explained what the detail suggests or proves?
  • Have I avoided claiming more than the evidence allows?
  • Is my response clear and grammatically complete?
